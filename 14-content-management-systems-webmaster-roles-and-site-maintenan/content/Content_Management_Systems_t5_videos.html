Introduction

Launching a website is not the finish line — it is the start of an ongoing operational commitment. Website maintenance and production management covers everything that happens after go-live: applying software and plugin updates, taking and testing backups, watching for intrusions, keeping pages fast, auditing content and links, responding to incidents, and telling clients what you are doing and when. For a CMS-based site in particular, where core software, themes, and third-party plugins all update on their own schedules, neglected maintenance is one of the most common causes of defacement, data loss, and downtime.

In this course, this topic connects the technical skills you have built to the professional role of a webmaster. You are expected to think like the person responsible for a live production site: what gets checked daily, weekly, and monthly; what you do before you touch production; how you recover when something breaks; and how you communicate planned work and outages to a non-technical client.

The three videos below approach the subject from different angles — a hands-on CMS maintenance checklist, a conceptual distinction between backup and disaster recovery, and a practical look at hardening a site against automated attacks. As you watch, note the recurring themes: staging environments before production changes, backups that are actually tested and stored off-site, recovery objectives expressed in time and data loss, and the difference between a one-time fix and a repeatable scheduled routine. Try to capture each recommended task into a draft maintenance calendar you could hand to a client.

Video 2: Disaster Recovery vs. Backup: What's the difference?

IBM Technology · 9:32 · 159,139 views · 5y ago · captions — Watch on YouTube

About this video

An IBM Technology explainer that separates two terms people often use interchangeably: backup (copies of data you can restore) and disaster recovery (a plan and infrastructure for resuming operations after a major failure). Expect discussion of planning concepts such as recovery objectives, what each approach protects against, and why having backups alone is not a recovery plan.

Why this video

It supplies the vocabulary and conceptual framing behind the backup and disaster recovery subtopic, from an established enterprise technology producer, so your maintenance plan can justify choices rather than just list tasks.
