Introduction

A webmaster is the person who owns a live website after launch. The role goes well beyond writing code: it includes keeping software patched, monitoring uptime and security, auditing content, coordinating with designers, developers, and content authors, and communicating clearly with clients and stakeholders about what is happening to their site and why. On small teams one person may do all of this; on larger teams the webmaster is often the coordinator who makes sure nothing falls through the cracks.

This topic matters in COP4813 because the technical skills you have built only create value if someone maintains them responsibly over time. Professional accountability — following platform guidelines, respecting search engine and accessibility standards, disclosing problems honestly, and staying current as tools change — is part of the job description, not an optional extra.

The three videos below approach the role from the operational side: the published standards a webmaster is expected to follow, the free diagnostic tools that support routine monitoring, and the audit habit that turns monitoring data into decisions. As you watch, note which tasks are recurring (daily, weekly, monthly), which require a judgment call you would need to explain to a client, and which could be documented as a checklist for a team to follow.

About this video

This short video covers Google's rebranding of the long-standing Webmaster Guidelines as Google Search Essentials, and explains what the renamed documentation says. Based on the title and description, it walks through the key requirements and best practices Google expects site owners to meet, and highlights what changed in the update. It is essentially a tour of the rulebook a webmaster is accountable to when running a public, indexed site.

Why this video

Professional standards are a core subtopic here, and this video points to the single most widely referenced set of published expectations for site owners. It also models the habit of continuous learning: guidelines get renamed and revised, and a webmaster is responsible for noticing.

Video 2: How to Use Microsoft Bing Webmaster Tools to Increase Your SEO Traffic

TM Blast · 9:03 · 5,506 views · 2y ago · captions — Watch on YouTube

About this video

TM Blast demonstrates Microsoft Bing Webmaster Tools and how to use it to monitor and improve search traffic. Expect a practical walkthrough of the dashboard — submitting and verifying a site, reviewing indexing and crawl information, and reading the reports the tool provides. The presenter frames it around real client work, noting that Bing accounts for a meaningful share of his search traffic.

Why this video

It shows the day-to-day operational side of the role: logging into a monitoring tool, reading diagnostics, and acting on them. It also reinforces an accountability point — a webmaster is responsible for all channels that reach the site, not just the most obvious one.

About this video

Part of a website audit series, this video focuses on WordPress sites and on benchmarks you can use to judge how a site is performing relative to competitors. It introduces the basic measures an auditor looks at and how to interpret whether the numbers are healthy. The emphasis is on establishing a baseline rather than on advanced or specialized analysis.

Why this video

Content and performance auditing is explicitly part of the webmaster's recurring duties, and this video gives a concrete, beginner-accessible starting point. Benchmark data is also what you present to a client when justifying maintenance work, which ties directly to professional communication.
