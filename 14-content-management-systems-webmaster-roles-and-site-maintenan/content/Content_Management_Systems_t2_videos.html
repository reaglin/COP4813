Introduction

A content management system (CMS) separates the job of creating and organizing content from the job of writing code. Instead of hand-editing HTML files, site owners log into an administrative dashboard, type content into forms, and the platform stores it in a database and renders it through templates. This single idea underpins a large share of the modern web, and it explains why so many professional web projects begin with the question "which CMS?" rather than "which framework?"

The platforms in this topic occupy different points on a spectrum. WordPress dominates by market share and plugin ecosystem; Joomla sits in a flexible middle tier; Drupal targets enterprise and government sites with complex content models and permissions; Shopify specializes in e-commerce; Wix and Squarespace bundle hosting, templates, and editing into a single hosted service; and headless CMS platforms strip away the front end entirely, delivering content through APIs to whatever application consumes it. Each choice trades control against convenience, and cost against capability.

The videos below approach the topic from the comparison angle and from the headless angle. As you watch, build a mental table: who is the intended user, what technical skill is assumed, where does the content actually live, who hosts it, and what happens when the project grows or the requirements change. Pay particular attention to the arguments made for traditional CMS platforms as well as against them—being able to argue both sides is the point of this module, not picking a favorite.
