Introduction

Almost every web project begins with the same question: do we build this on an existing content management system, or do we write it from scratch? A CMS such as WordPress, Drupal, Joomla, or Shopify gives you a pre-built administrative interface, a content database, a theming layer, and a plugin ecosystem on day one. A custom build gives you complete control over the architecture, data model, and performance profile, but you are responsible for every piece of functionality a CMS would have handed you for free. Between these two poles sit hybrid options — headless CMS platforms and code-first frameworks — that let you keep an editing interface while building the front end yourself.

This question matters in COP4813 because choosing the wrong approach is expensive and hard to reverse. The decision is rarely about which technology is "better"; it is about matching the tool to the project's scope, the available budget, the expected traffic and growth, the client's willingness and ability to administer the site, and the long-term security and maintenance burden someone will have to carry. A small business brochure site and a high-traffic application with a custom data model call for very different answers, and a professional should be able to explain why.

As you watch the videos below, listen for the decision criteria rather than the product recommendations. Note when a presenter says a tool is a poor fit and what reason they give — those negative cases are often more instructive than the sales pitch. Try to collect the criteria you hear into a checklist you could actually apply to a client scenario: scope, budget, editorial workflow, scalability, technical capability of the people who will run the site, and ongoing maintenance and security responsibility.
