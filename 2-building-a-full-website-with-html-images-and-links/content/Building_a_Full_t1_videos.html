Introduction

A multi-page website is simply a collection of HTML documents that live together in an organized folder and are connected to each other through links. Instead of putting everything on one long page, you separate content into purposeful pages — a home page, an about page, a contact page — and give visitors a consistent way to move between them. Before you write any HTML, though, you need a plan: what pages exist, what each page is for, what files and folders hold them, and how the navigation ties it all together.

In this course, this topic is the foundation for everything else in Module 2. HTML is the structural layer of the web: elements like header, nav, main, section, article, and footer describe what each part of a page means, not just how it looks. Semantic markup matters because it makes your pages easier for other developers to read, easier for search engines to interpret, and far more usable for people relying on screen readers. Internal links, written as relative paths, are what turn a pile of separate HTML files into a single coherent site.

The videos below focus on the planning and structuring habits that come before and around the code. As you watch, look for the ideas you can transfer directly to a website project: how to break a project into pages or components, how to decide on a folder and file organization, how to sketch structure before building, and how to keep naming and layout consistent. The specific tools and languages shown may differ from plain HTML, but the planning discipline is the same.
