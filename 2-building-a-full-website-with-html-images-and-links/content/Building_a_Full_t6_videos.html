Introduction

Linking outward is what makes your site part of the web rather than an island. When you point to a page on another site, you need an absolute URL — the full address including the scheme (https://), the domain, and any path — because a relative path would only ever be resolved against your own site's folders. This topic covers how to write those links with the anchor element and how to choose attributes that affect where the page opens and how safely.

Two attributes matter most here. The target attribute (commonly target="_blank") controls whether the destination replaces the current page or opens in a new tab, and historically a new tab could give the destination limited scripting access back to your page, which is why rel="noopener noreferrer" is the standard companion. The rel attribute also describes the relationship between your page and the destination more broadly, with values such as nofollow, external, and license. Alongside the technical details, there is a user-experience obligation: link text should tell people where they are going, and users should not be surprised by a tab opening unexpectedly.

As you watch, focus on three things: the exact anatomy of the anchor tag (href, text content, closing tag), how the videos distinguish absolute from relative URLs, and any mention of target or rel. Note that short beginner tutorials often stop before the security discussion — treat the module readings as the authority on rel="noopener noreferrer" and use the videos to build fluency with the markup itself.
