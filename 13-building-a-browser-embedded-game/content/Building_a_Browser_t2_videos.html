Introduction

The HTML5 Canvas element gives you a blank rectangle of pixels in the browser and a JavaScript drawing context (usually obtained with getContext('2d')) that lets you paint on it. Everything a player sees in a Canvas game — the background, the character, the obstacles, the score text — is drawn by your code, one shape or image at a time, and then erased and drawn again a fraction of a second later. There is no DOM element for the player's character; there is only what you drew on the most recent frame.

In this module that matters because Canvas drawing is the output half of your game. You will set up the canvas and its size, establish the coordinate grid (origin at the top-left, x increasing to the right, y increasing downward), layer a background, draw rectangles and images for obstacles and objects, and cut frames out of a sprite sheet with drawImage to animate a character. Then you will clear the canvas with clearRect and redraw everything so that motion appears smooth rather than smeared.

As you watch the videos below, pay attention to a few recurring details: how the canvas is created and sized in HTML versus in JavaScript, how each drawing call translates numbers into positions on screen, the order in which things are drawn (background first, foreground last), and where the clear-and-redraw step sits inside the animation loop. Try pausing and recreating small pieces in your own file — a filled rectangle, an image at a given x and y — before attempting a full game.

How These Videos Support the Learning Outcomes

Together these videos build the drawing layer that the module's outcomes depend on.
